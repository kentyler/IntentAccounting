Explanation · motivating argument

Why Intent Accounting?

The new capability is not merely that machines can produce more text. It is that a machine can become a persistent reader of an organization’s inscriptions.

The reader changes the value of the books

For centuries, disciplined bookkeeping was expensive to write and expensive to read. A merchant who knew the business personally could often live without a complete formal account of it. The calculation changed when absent owners, creditors, courts, tax authorities, and auditors needed records that could travel and be held to account.

Language models introduce another absent reader. They can read across documents, conversations, tickets, records, and long spans of time. They can also help produce structured entries at a cost that would previously have made such inscription impractical.

The value of the books is not the writing by itself. It is the answerability the writing makes possible for a reader who was not present when the work happened.

Being able to read everything is not enough

An organization already produces a mountain of language. That does not make the organization legible. Existing records are selective: commitments remain in meetings, impossibilities are omitted, projections are presented as facts, and abandoned decisions disappear into chat history.

An LLM pointed at that material may become a fluent reader of the organization’s self-description. Fluency does not make omissions answerable. A useful ledger must therefore specify the form in which claims, evidence, acceptance, refusal, and correction enter the record.

The unit changes from money to intent

Money is a powerful compression. It makes heterogeneous activity comparable at the boundary where the organization meets markets, taxation, ownership, and law. But that compression deletes much of what organizes the interior: what was intended, who undertook it, what evidence was offered, what remained contested, and who accepted responsibility.

Intent Accounting treats intentions and commitments as the primary accounting units. Financial views can still be derived when needed; they are no longer presumed to be the only books that matter.

From inspection to answerability

The system does not promise that every artifact can be exhaustively inspected. Instead it asks a scalable accounting question: who answers for this entry, under what authority, and what remains visible if it is dishonored or reversed?

This is particularly important for LLM participation. A model cannot be made financially liable in the same way as a legal person. It can, however, be given a named role, accumulate a record, carry open commitments, present work, accept responsibility within a grant, and lose standing or authority in ways recorded by the books.

The thesis

Adoption follows the reader. When a reader arrives who can use a richer record, the organization can afford to keep books of the commitments that previously survived only in memory, custom, and informal conversation.
