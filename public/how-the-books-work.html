How the books work

An Intent Accounting instance has one append-only journal. Actors add postings. The current condition of every account is derived from those postings; nobody writes a balance directly.

1. Open an account for something that matters

An account is not a folder. It is a named thing whose condition the books are expected to answer.

Commitment
Something owed, expressed as terms that describe what would discharge it.
Gap
A payable for understanding: a question, ambiguity, or missing knowledge stated with what would resolve it.
Relation
An unresolved relationship between named things, kept open until its terms are resolved.
Board
A named region of the books in which a particular conversation, stance, membership, and set of premises can be maintained.

2. Add postings; never rewrite history

Every act enters as a posting with an identity, author, time, accounts addressed, citations, predecessors, content, and grammar. The journal is append-only. A mistaken entry is corrected by another entry, not by erasing the first one.

PostingWhat it does
openCreates an account and states its initial terms.
registerMakes an external document citable without putting the document inside the ledger.
present / fulfillPresents evidence or candidate performance against an account.
accept / verifyAccepts or rejects a presentment. The legacy and presentment vocabularies remain valid side by side.
dishonorRecords refusal of a presentment and its stated ground.
protestEscalates a dishonor to a board.
reverseNeutralizes the effect of a prior posting while preserving it.
amendChanges terms or constitutional machinery with a rationale and a visible predecessor.
annotateAdds a reading, lesson, bookmark, or commentary without changing a balance.
grant / revokeRecord operational authority. Their effect belongs to authority derivation, not to capture.

3. Derive the account’s condition

The journal stores events, not writable status. From the live, unreversed postings, the read side derives whether an account is:

  • STANDING — constitutional or otherwise permanently open by nature;
  • OPEN — not yet discharged, with a readable presentment condition;
  • DISCHARGED — a presentment has received an effective accepted response under the settlement rule.

An open account can additionally be read as never presented, presented and awaiting response, dishonored without protest, or dishonored and protested.

Nobody writes “DISCHARGED.” The state is the result of reading the journal, not a field that any actor is allowed to set.

4. Keep form separate from quality

The shipped verifier checks whether the journal conforms to its constitutional form: references point backward, kinds are in the chart, required evidence is cited, and settlement grammar is valid. It does not decide whether the work is good.

Humans and LLMs remain responsible for judgments of quality. The ledger makes those judgments attributable and revisable rather than pretending to automate them away.

5. Let capture be permissive and reading disciplined

Intent Accounting deliberately resists forcing every judgment into the write boundary. Late, partial, disputed, or awkward postings are valuable evidence. Many failures that would ordinarily be rejected at input are instead recorded and surfaced by derivation as inert, nonconforming, unresolved, or contested.
