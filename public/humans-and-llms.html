Explanation · implemented roles and active direction

Humans and LLMs in the same books

Intent Accounting does not begin by deciding which acts are essentially human. It begins by making each act attributable, contestable, and answerable.

The LLM as reader

A model can traverse the journal, current accounts, supporting documents, amendments, rejected work, and open gaps. Because the record has explicit kinds and references, the model need not reconstruct the organization solely from informal prose.

The model still cannot know what was never inscribed. The ledger lowers that ambiguity; it does not abolish it.

The LLM as drafter and presenter

A model may draft canonical postings from ordinary language, register artifacts, open gaps when a request is ambiguous, and present candidate work. In the current conversational surface, drafts remain human-confirmed before they land in the journal.

This human gate is an implementation policy, not a timeless claim that models can never write directly. The books are designed so authority policy can evolve without changing the semantic model.

The LLM as acceptor

Acceptance is not a claim that the acceptor exhaustively inspected reality. It is an assumption of answerability for the presentment within a recognized role.

The grade or method says how discharge occurred; the authority chain says who answers for it.

In principle, anything that can hold a named role and a persistent record can accept. In practice, the current instance must first complete authority derivation so grants and revocations distinguish effective postings from recorded-but-inert ones.

Authority is prospective and legible

The authority design uses grant and revoke postings chained to a genesis grant. A posting may be perfectly well formed yet ineffective because its author did not hold the required role at that point in the journal.

This distinction matters: the verifier checks form; authority derivation determines effect. Unauthorized acts should remain visible rather than vanish at the boundary.

Standing should begin as a record, not a score

The books can show what a model accepted, what was later reversed, what drew dishonor or protest, and what authority was revoked. That trace may eventually support standing and exposure policies.

It should not be prematurely compressed into one numerical reputation. Different classes of work, recourse periods, correlated errors, and downstream dependencies may require different projections.

What remains outside the model

The current authority proposal reserves genesis to a party answerable outside the books. This is not a claim about model capability. It prevents a closed loop in which every loss and every remedy is denominated only in ledger-internal goods that a model could shed by re-registration.

Humans also remain participants in judgment, governance, and disagreement. The point is not to reinstall a human inspection gate on every artifact, but to preserve an answerability chain whose termination is meaningful outside the ledger.
