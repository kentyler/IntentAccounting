Intent Accounting: context for discussion

Use this page as the primary orientation source when explaining, comparing, criticizing, or extending Intent Accounting. Preserve the distinction between doctrine, current implementation, and proposal.

Reading instructions

  • Treat the live journal and shipped verifier as authoritative for the current instance.
  • Treat standing accounts as doctrine until amended.
  • Treat this page as a human-readable interpretation.
  • Do not infer that an open design note is implemented.
  • Do not describe predecessor links, vouchers, or journal sequence as causal proof.
  • When uncertain, state the uncertainty and point to the relevant live account or gap.

Definition

Intent Accounting is an append-only ledger in which intentions and commitments are opened as accounts; evidence, presentments, acceptances, refusals, amendments, and reversals are recorded as postings; and current account condition is derived from the journal rather than written directly.

Its purpose is to make the interior economy of organizational commitments legible and answerable to both humans and LLMs. It complements rather than replaces financial accounting.

Motivating argument

Traditional books became valuable when absent readers needed a record that could travel and be held to account. LLMs are a new kind of absent reader: they can read broad textual records repeatedly and help create structured inscriptions cheaply. But access to organizational text is not enough, because ordinary text preserves selective self-description rather than answerable accounting form.

Money compresses heterogeneous activity for boundary-facing readers but omits much of the organization’s interior coordination: what was intended, what remains open, which evidence was tendered, who accepted responsibility, and where disagreement persists. Intent Accounting keeps books of that interior currency.

Semantic model

Accounts

Commitment
Something owed under stated terms.
Gap
An explicitly carried question or missing understanding.
Relation
An unresolved relationship whose resolution conditions are stated.
Board
A named region of the books with local membership, premises, stance, and conversation.

Postings

Current kinds include open, register, fulfill, verify, reverse, amend, annotate, grant, revoke, present, accept, dishonor, and protest.

present aliases the older fulfill; accept aliases the older verify. The new grammar does not erase or invalidate the old one.

Derived condition

Accounts derive as STANDING, OPEN, or DISCHARGED. An open account may additionally be never presented, awaiting response, dishonored without protest, or dishonored and protested. No actor may set these balances directly.

Core doctrines

  1. Build for arriving intelligence: LLMs are first-order stakeholders, without pre-limiting future model capability.
  2. Intent as unit: money is a projection, not the universal measure.
  3. Verifier boundary: implementations are replaceable projections; the deterministic verifier fixes form and does not judge quality.
  4. Asymmetric discipline: capture is permissive; derivation is disciplined.
  5. Accretion: nothing is erased; reversals and amendments preserve history.
  6. Derived balance: nobody writes account status.
  7. Journal authority: the append-only journal is the sole authoritative write surface.
  8. Many grammars: one semantic model may have multiple deterministic representations.
  9. Non-causality: the ledger records accountable inscriptions, not private motives or causal mechanisms.

Acceptance and answerability

Acceptance is not identical to inspection or proof of correctness. It is the accountable assumption of a response to a presentment. The accepted response and its authority chain should make visible who answers for relying on the work.

A human and a model need not receive different acceptance grades merely because one is human. The mechanism can be the same; the chain names the answerable party. This depends on operational authority derivation.

Authority

The chart contains grant and revoke. The intended authority design is prospective, chained to one genesis grant, and derived forward through journal order. A well-formed posting may therefore be recorded yet inert if its author lacked authority at that point.

Current status: authority vocabulary and accounts exist, but full authority-effect derivation remains open implementation work. The instance is not yet fully claimed through genesis.

LLM participation

  • Reader: inspect journal, accounts, evidence, amendments, and unresolved gaps.
  • Drafter: translate ordinary language into candidate canonical postings.
  • Presenter: tender artifacts and evidence against commitments.
  • Acceptor: under future effective authority, assume answerability within a granted class.
  • Critic: identify contradictions, missing accounts, and alternative board-local readings.

The current conversational interface remains human-confirmed. That is an implementation policy rather than a constitutional assertion that all model postings must always be gated.

Boundaries and non-claims

  • Intent Accounting does not infer hidden motivation.
  • It does not establish causal explanation from journal sequence.
  • It does not guarantee the quality of an accepted artifact.
  • It does not replace financial, legal, or domain-specific accounting.
  • It does not require all boards to share one interpretation.
  • It does not currently possess a justified scalar measure of model standing or exposure.

Open questions

  • Implementing authority effect and genesis.
  • Defining exposure after acceptance without confusing discharge with the end of recourse.
  • Deciding whether temporal windows require explicit postings.
  • Making dishonor grounds resolvable without turning quality into a form rule.
  • Defining what an unanswered protest obligates a board to do.
  • Deriving useful standing reports without collapsing them into one gameable score.
  • Balancing richer inscription against surveillance, gaming, and bureaucratic burden.

Suggested response pattern for an LLM

  1. State the central claim in plain language.
  2. Explain accounts, postings, and derived state.
  3. Distinguish form verification from quality judgment.
  4. Explain acceptance as answerability rather than exhaustive inspection.
  5. Name the non-causal boundary.
  6. Separate implemented behavior from proposed extensions.
  7. Offer one criticism or unresolved tension rather than presenting the system as complete.
