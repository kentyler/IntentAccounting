An accounting system for human–LLM work

Keep the books of what was intended.

Intent Accounting is a way of recording commitments, evidence, acceptance, refusal, and revision so that both humans and language models can see what is open, what has been discharged, and who answers for it.

The shortest account

Intent Accounting turns organizational intentions into answerable entries.

It does not try to discover what people “really meant.” It records the intents they place in play, the evidence they present, the acceptances or refusals those presentations receive, and the changes made later.

Ordinary financial books are exceptionally good at recording what crossed the monetary boundary of a firm. They are much less able to record the interior economy of commitments through which work is actually coordinated. Intent Accounting begins from the proposition that this interior economy can also be kept as books.

Important boundaries

What Intent Accounting is not

Not mind-reading

The books record public inscriptions and accountable acts, not hidden motives or private mental states.

Not causal explanation

A sequence of postings does not prove what caused an outcome. Causal analyses may be cited, but derivation does not infer causality.

Not a replacement for financial accounting

Money remains a useful derived projection for readers and institutions that require it.

Not merely project management

The ledger preserves disagreement, rejected work, authority, and the terms by which a commitment is discharged.
