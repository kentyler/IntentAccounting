Explanation · illustrative example

One commitment through the books

A small example shows the difference between doing work, presenting it, and making the acceptance of it answerable.

The scenario

A team wants a migration script that moves customer records to a new database without losing identifiers. An LLM is asked to produce the script. Another actor will decide whether to accept responsibility for relying on it.

The entries

  1. Open the commitment.
    MIG-17 is opened with terms: preserve every customer identifier, provide a dry-run report, and pass the registered migration test suite.
  2. Register the evidence source.
    The test suite and schema specification are registered as citable documents. The documents remain outside the journal; the journal records their resolvable locations.
  3. Present the work.
    The producing LLM posts present-MIG-17-v1, cites the script, dry-run report, and test output, and addresses MIG-17.
  4. Respond to the presentment.
    An authorized acceptor may accept it, reject it, or dishonor it with a ground. Merely uploading the script does not discharge the commitment.
  5. Derive the condition.
    Before response, the account is OPEN and presented_awaiting. After an effective accepted response, it is DISCHARGED. Nobody writes that state directly.

Path A: acceptance

The acceptor reviews what its role requires and posts an accepted response referencing the presentment. The books now derive the commitment as discharged and preserve both the presenter and acceptor.

If the script later proves defective, the acceptance or related entries can be reversed. The original acceptance remains visible, along with the rationale for reversing it.

Path B: dishonor and protest

Suppose the dry-run report omits archived customers. The responder posts a dishonor with that ground. The commitment remains open and derives as dishonored_unprotested.

The presenter may revise and present again. Alternatively, a party may protest the dishonor to a board, making the disagreement visible in a named conversational region. The board does not magically reveal the correct answer; it provides a place in the books for the dispute and its premises.

What this example makes visible

  • The terms existed before the candidate solution.
  • The solution and its cited evidence are distinct from its acceptance.
  • Acceptance names an answerable actor; it is not a hidden state in a workflow tool.
  • Refusal is an entry with ground, not silence.
  • Correction preserves the failed path rather than replacing history.
  • A later LLM can reconstruct what was attempted and where judgment entered.

A simplified canonical sketch

{"id":"open-MIG-17","kind":"open","author":"team",...}
{"id":"present-MIG-17-v1","kind":"present","author":"model-A", "accounts":["MIG-17"], "vouchers":["D-script-v1","D-test-run-v1"],...}
{"id":"accept-MIG-17-v1","kind":"accept","author":"reviewer-B", "accounts":["MIG-17"], "predecessors":["present-MIG-17-v1"], "content":{"verdict":"accepted"},...}

This is deliberately incomplete. The canonical form has nine required fields, and the verifier—not this illustration—is authoritative about conformance.
