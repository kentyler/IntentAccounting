# Projection and Recognition

*Draft doctrinal text. Intended to enter the build instructions as a new section
(after Authority, before Claiming), implemented as a chart-level amendment with
doctrine commitments, following the pattern of Boards (§7) and Authority (§8).*

---

## The problem these doctrines address

The existing stack records what happened and whether it was answered. The anchor
tells you that an intent was addressed; red and green register outcome; the
constitution governs capture, derivation, and the append-only record. The
doctrines that exist -- boards, authority, stance -- govern locality, permission,
and posture. None of them governs the transition from *this worked here* to *this
should work there*.

That transition is projection, and it is the most consequential act in the books.
A local green, honestly anchored, becomes the warrant for a rollout, a policy, a
commitment of resources across occasions the firm has not yet met. If the green
was accidental, contextual, or non-repeating, the rollout fails -- but the
failure arrives later, elsewhere, distributed across the occasions that trusted
the projection. The books as currently constituted record the failure faithfully
when it arrives (the reds accumulate), but they do not require the projection
itself to be recorded as an accountable act, and they do not ask whether the
green had earned the right to travel.

Two doctrines are needed. **Projection** makes the act of extending an outcome
beyond its occasion a first-class, accountable posting. **Recognition** governs
when a green has earned the right to be projected, at what scope, and under what
conditions.

---

## Projection

A **projection** is the act of drawing on an occasion-specific outcome to warrant
action in occasions not yet encountered. It is the claim that what worked here
will work there -- where "there" names a scope (a different team, site,
population, time horizon, or set of conditions) that the original occasion did
not cover.

Projection is not repetition. Repeating an intervention in the same setting,
under the same conditions, with the same actors, draws on the anchor directly.
The occasion has not changed; the green covers it. Projection begins at the point
where the new occasion differs from the anchored one in ways that could matter --
and the books must record the claim that they do not.

### What the doctrine requires

**PJ-1. Projection is a recorded act.** Every projection enters the books as a
posting. It is not implicit in a rollout, a resource allocation, or a repeated
intervention at a different site. The act of claiming that a green travels is
itself a commitment -- the books can charge it, verify it, or reverse it. A
projection that is never posted is invisible to the books; if it fails, the
failure arrives as surprise. The discipline of posting it converts silent
fragility into legible fragility.

**PJ-2. A projection cites its ground and names its scope.** The posting must
reference (in vouchers or predecessors) the outcome(s) it draws from -- the
green(s) that warrant the claim. It must name the scope it claims to cover: what
occasions, populations, conditions, or settings the projection asserts the
outcome will hold across. A projection without cited ground is an unsupported
commitment; a projection without stated scope is an unbounded claim. Both are
well-formed (the journal accepts them -- P-4) but legibly deficient on the read
side.

**PJ-3. A projection creates a generalization liability.** The moment a
projection is posted, it owes the books a warrant for why the green should
travel. This liability is not discharged by the projection itself -- stating that
something will work elsewhere does not explain why. The liability sits open until
a recognition posting discharges it. A projection whose liability remains open is
not prohibited (the books do not refuse postings -- P-4), but its status is
legible: it is an undischarged claim, the epistemic analogue of spending revenue
not yet earned.

**PJ-4. The occasion boundary is a first-class object.** The distinction between
"same occasion" and "new occasion" is not implicit. What constitutes a materially
different occasion depends on what the intervention touches: a change of site may
matter for a logistics practice but not for a software pattern; a change of
population may matter for a medical intervention but not for an accounting
procedure. The projection posting is where the claim about occasion boundaries
lives. The books do not decide what counts as a boundary; the projector asserts
it, and the assertion is accountable.

### What projection is not

Projection is not prediction. A prediction says "X will happen"; a projection
says "the warrant for X, anchored here, covers there." Prediction is about the
future state of the world; projection is about the scope of an understanding. The
books may contain predictions (as commitments with terms), but the Projection
Doctrine governs something narrower: the claimed transportability of an anchored
result.

Projection is not the Projection Doctrine's invention. People project constantly
-- every rollout, every best-practice transfer, every "this worked for them so
we'll do it too" is a projection. The doctrine does not create projection; it
makes it visible, citable, and chargeable in the books.

## Recognition

An anchored outcome is recognized first as an event of its occasion. It is a fact
about what happened, where, under what conditions, with what result. This
occasion-level recognition is automatic: the green posting itself is the
recognition. No further act is needed to say "this worked here."

The question Recognition addresses is different: **when may a green be treated as
transportable understanding -- something the books can capitalize and draw
against in a wider scope?**

The accounting analogy is revenue recognition. The probe succeeded -- that is
cash in the door, undeniable, anchored. But recognizing that success as
knowledge the organization can draw against in a rollout requires the earning
process to be complete. The earning process is the warrant. Until it is complete,
the green sits in deferred understanding -- the epistemic analogue of unearned
revenue. Scaling on an undischarged green is spending revenue not yet earned.

### What the doctrine requires

**RD-1. The recognition rule.** A projection (PJ-3) creates a generalization
liability. The liability is discharged only by a warranted account of the
conditions under which the outcome may travel. Such an account is called a
**transport warrant**. Recognition authorizes projection only within the scope
for which the liability has been discharged. No recognition, no authorized
projection -- but the books still record both the green and the unrecognized
projection (P-4, P-5). The deficiency is legible, not prevented.

**RD-2. The transport warrant.** A warrant is not a mechanism in the strict
causal sense, though a mechanism is one especially strong form. What the doctrine
requires is a sufficiently constrained account of:

- **why** the outcome may travel (generative mechanism, enabling constraints,
  boundary conditions, reliable invariance, causal disposition, or pattern of
  interactions -- the form is not prescribed);
- **where** the outcome may travel (the scope and the boundary conditions that
  define its limits);
- **what would show that it has failed to travel** (the conditions under which
  the warrant is falsified -- the reds it is exposed to).

The warrant must accept answerability. It may not prescribe its own form --
mechanism, narrative, statistical regularity, structural argument -- but it must
expose itself to correction.

**RD-3. The cross-posting requirement.** A transport warrant must post
consequences to accounts other than the green from which it was inferred. If it
genuinely explains why the intervention worked, it must constrain other intents --
predict which adjacent postings should go red, forbid certain projections,
entangle with commitments elsewhere in the ledger. A warrant that balances
exactly one green, touching nothing else, is a confirmation loop. Confabulated
warrants are cheap precisely because they are local; real ones are expensive
because they create liabilities all over the books.

This is the native IA test for explanatory adequacy. It does not ask "does this
sound right?" (which an LLM will always answer yes). It asks "does accepting this
change what the organization must expect, prohibit, test, or remain answerable
for elsewhere?" A warrant that entails nothing beyond the green it explains has
not earned recognition.

**RD-4. Temporal exposure.** A warrant must create constraints before the
relevant outcomes are known. It must post predictions, exclusions, boundary
conditions, or commitments that can subsequently go red. It must reduce the
future freedom of the explanation.

This is what makes a real warrant expensive. A confabulation says "here is why
that happened" -- always after the fact, always fitting the evidence, never
risking anything. A recognized warrant says "because that happened for these
reasons, here is what should and should not happen next -- and the ledger may
charge this account if it does not."

The test: a warrant discharges a generalization liability only when it creates
ex ante commitments in independently anchored accounts and survives the occasions
in which those commitments are exposed. Survival is not permanent -- a warrant
remains answerable to every future occasion within its scope.

**RD-5. Recognition is always scoped.** Recognition never converts a contingent
account into settled knowledge without qualification. It is not a binary property
of a warrant. It is a relation among:

> outcome + warrant + scope + boundary conditions + exposed consequences.

A warrant might discharge the liability for one projection while remaining wholly
inadequate for another. Evidence that justifies applying a practice to a second
shift in the same factory may not justify applying it across industries.

Recognition is stated at a level:

- recognized for this process and team;
- recognized across these specified sites;
- recognized while stated enabling constraints remain present;
- provisionally recognized pending a deliberately dissimilar trial;
- not recognized for populations outside the observed range.

When a recognized warrant conflicts with a later anchor, the anchor wins. The
warrant is revised, its scope narrowed, or its recognition reversed. Recognition
is a derived status -- the authoritative trace remains primary.

### The relation between projection and recognition

Projection without recognition is permitted but legibly deficient. The books
record it, the liability is visible, and anyone reading the ledger can see that
the projection is drawing on understanding that has not yet been earned. This is
not a blocking rule -- it is an accounting rule. The organization may choose to
act on an unrecognized projection (urgency, opportunity cost, acceptable risk),
but the books show what it is doing: spending unearned revenue.

Recognition without projection is inert. A warrant discharged against a green
that is never projected has no organizational effect -- the understanding sits
recognized but unused. This is not a deficiency; it is knowledge in reserve.

The sequence:

1. **Green accrues** -- the outcome is anchored to its occasion.
2. **Projection creates liability** -- someone claims the green travels.
3. **Warrant is posted** -- an account of why, where, and what would falsify.
4. **Warrant is exposed** -- the ex ante commitments face new occasions.
5. **Recognition is earned** -- the warrant has survived exposure at stated scope.
6. **Projection draws on recognition** -- the books authorize the draw at that scope.

The compressed form:

> Outcomes accrue. Projection creates liability. Warrant discharges it.
> Recognition is always scoped.

Or, in the ledger's own idiom:

> Greens accrue; projections owe; warrants constrain;
> only recognized understanding scales.

### What recognition does to failure

Without a transport warrant, repeated reds map the local terrain. They tell us
where an intervention did and did not work, but they do not tell us what
structure generated the distribution.

Once a warrant has been posted, reds acquire a second role. They are no longer
only failed probes. They are charges against particular elements of the warrant:

- the warrant may be wrong;
- a boundary condition may have been omitted;
- the occasion may belong to a different class than assumed;
- the proposed scope may be too broad;
- the intervention may not have instantiated the warrant's conditions as claimed.

The failure record moves from being a map of outcomes to being a process for
decomposing and revising the warrant. The warrant makes failure more informative
because it gives the red somewhere specific to post. This completes the
failure-optimization argument: nine failed probes as radar pulse gives you a map
of the environment; nine failed probes against a posted warrant gives you a
decomposition of the warrant.

### Natural-science imports

When a warrant draws on biology, physics, or any natural science, the import
enters the books as content -- a description, with provenance, fully subject to
the same accountability as all content. Natural science is not a second anchor.
Its claims arrive from a ledger (the scientific literature) in which more
liabilities have been exposed and discharged, but the import is not truth from
outside discourse. It is borrowed warrant from another book.

An imported warrant should carry:

- its source and provenance;
- the scope in which it has previously been warranted;
- the analogy or transduction by which it is being applied here;
- the differences that could break that transduction;
- the new commitments created by using it in this context.

The firm is not importing truth. It is borrowing warrant, and it becomes
answerable for the terms of the borrowing.

## Relation to existing doctrines

**P-4 (capture is undisciplined).** Projection and Recognition are entirely
read-side. An unrecognized projection, a warrant that touches nothing else, a
projection without cited ground -- all are well-formed and accepted by the
journal. The deficiency is derived, never a capture-time rejection.

**P-5 (nothing is erased).** A reversed recognition remains in the books. A
warrant whose scope was narrowed retains its original claim. The history of what
was believed transportable and why is itself accountable.

**P-7 (journal is sole write surface).** Recognition status is derived from the
journal. There is no stored "recognized" flag. Whether a green has been
recognized at a given scope is computed by walking the projection, warrant, and
exposure chain forward.

**Boards (§7).** A projection's scope often aligns with board boundaries -- the
scope "this team" or "this supplier" corresponds to a board. But projection scope
and board membership are independent: a warrant may cover multiple boards or a
fraction of one.

**Authority (§8).** Who may post a projection or a recognition is governed by
authority. The recorder role suffices for projection (it is an open + annotate
pattern). Whether recognition requires verifier-level authority -- whether
discharging a generalization liability is an act that needs distinct-author
verification -- is owner policy, but the conservative default is yes: recognizing
understanding as transportable is a verification act, not a recording act.

---

## What the doctrine does not do

It does not require every green to produce a mechanism. A green that stays
attached to its occasion owes nothing. Many greens will never be projected, and
the books do not nag them for explanations.

It does not prescribe the form of a warrant. Mechanism, statistical regularity,
structural argument, enabling-constraint analysis -- the books accept any form
that meets the cross-posting and temporal-exposure tests. IA does not smuggle in
a particular epistemology.

It does not block action. An organization may act on an unrecognized projection.
The books record that it did so, and the record is legible. The doctrine converts
invisible risk into visible risk, not into prohibition.

It does not settle the question of what counts as a materially different occasion.
That judgment is the projector's, and it is accountable. Two reasonable people may
disagree about whether a new site is "the same occasion" or a "new occasion."
The books record the claim, not the truth of the boundary.
